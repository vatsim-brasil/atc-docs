## Overview

The Atlântico FIR covers the entire South Atlantic region, spanning almost 11 million square kilometers. The unit responsible for providing air traffic control service, as well as flight information and alerting service, to flights transiting this region is the Atlântico ACC (`SBAO_FSS`). The FIR has no radar coverage, either primary or secondary, so monitoring is carried out primarily through position reports at compulsory reporting points or at positions defined by latitude and longitude. Another system, called Automatic Dependent Surveillance – Contract[^1], is also used to monitor aircraft.

[^1]:Known in Portuguese as *Vigilância Automática Dependente – Contrato*, abbreviated ADS-C.

Communications between aircraft and the Atlântico ACC are carried out either by radio, using frequencies in the HF band (*High Frequency*), or by datalink, through dedicated equipment on board the aircraft.

The FIR can be divided into two operational profiles:

- a northern one, covering traffic flying between Brazil and Europe or North Africa (**EURSAM Corridor**); and
- a southern one, covering traffic flying between Brazil and Southern Africa (**AORRA**).

Given the absence of radar coverage and the lack of more accessible and clear communication between pilots and controllers, the control dynamics of the Atlântico ACC differ from those of the other FIRs in the country, all of which have radar and VHF (*Very High Frequency*) coverage. In addition, other routines specific to the control of oceanic regions[^2], such as the use of SELCAL (*Selective Calling*), mastery of alternative separation procedures and organized management of the information sent by pilots, are actively applied during control.

[^2]:Under the rules of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), the provisions for oceanic control may also be applied to remote continental FIRs, since they share the same characteristics as control in oceanic areas: absence of radar coverage and difficulties with VHF communication.
