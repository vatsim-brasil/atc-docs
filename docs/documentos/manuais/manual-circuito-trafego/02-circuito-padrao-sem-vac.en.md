# Standard (rectangular) circuit when there is no VAC

## Basic circuit geometry

Without published restrictions, use a simple rectangle around the runway:

| Leg | Function | Point of attention |
|---|---|---|
| Upwind leg (upwind) | Parallel to the runway, offset from the centreline, in the landing direction | avoid crossing/entering the opposite circuit |
| Crosswind (crosswind) | Transition to the circuit side | start the turn according to altitude/profile |
| Downwind leg (downwind) | Parallel to the runway, opposite to the landing direction | where the TWR organises the sequence |
| Base leg (base) | Final turn for alignment | adjust separation with the traffic ahead |
| Final | On the extended centreline, approach and landing | stabilisation, clearance, go-around |

## Standard direction per runway (no publication)

- **Left-hand turns** for the runway in use.
- If there is an operational reason (obstacles, city, noise, heliport, etc.), **this is a local restriction** and must be treated as a specific procedure (publication/instruction).

## Circuit side (practical notion)

- "Left-hand circuit RWY XX": the pilot makes all turns to the left.
- "Right-hand circuit RWY XX": the pilot makes all turns to the right.

## Runway in use (no VAC)

Recommended (practical) criteria:

- prioritise **headwind**, runway conditions, safety and flow.
- avoid frequent runway changes if this degrades the sequence and increases conflict.

## Example of a quick TWR briefing (standard)

- Runway in use: **RWY 16**
- Circuit: **right-hand**
- Reports: **downwind / base / final**
- Restrictions: **(if any)** "avoid overflying area X", "maintain 1000 ft <abbr title="Above Aerodrome Level">AAL</abbr> in the circuit", etc.

!!! tip "Local information"
    If local information appears (through any official means), replace the standard immediately.
