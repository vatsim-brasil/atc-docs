# Joining and leaving the circuit (TWR)

## Objective

Integrate aircraft into the circuit with predictability and visual separation.

## Joining the circuit (practical standard without VAC)

Typical options the TWR can use, in a staggered manner:

| Method | When to use | Advantages | Risks / precautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joining the **downwind leg** (join downwind) | most common standard | makes sequencing easier | avoid cutting base/final |
| Joining **base** | low and manageable traffic | reduces time in the circuit | risk of "cutting off" the traffic ahead |
| **Straight-in final** (straight-in) | when there is no conflict | fast | may conflict with the circuit; require spacing |
| **Orbit** / "remain" outside the circuit | saturation | safe | increases radio workload; define point/altitude |

## Leaving the circuit (departures)

After takeoff, the TWR may:

- keep **runway heading** up to an altitude and then release the turn towards the route,
- release an early/late turn to avoid circuit traffic.

| Departure type | When to use | Instruction example |
|---|---|---|
| Standard departure | light traffic | "after departure, proceed…" |
| Departure with heading restriction | traffic in the circuit | "maintain runway heading until …" |
| Departure via the crosswind leg | organise flow | "turn right/left crosswind…" |

## Critical point: crossing final/base

Without a VAC, treat this as a **higher-risk situation**:

- avoid authorising a crossing if there is traffic on final/base.
- if unavoidable, require a **visual report** and apply **time separation** (e.g. "cross after the landing traffic on final").

!!! warning "When in doubt"
    When in doubt, hold the traffic (hold/orbit) and reorder.
