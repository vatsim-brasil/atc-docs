# Introduction

## Overview

This manual has been consolidated in a **descriptive and operational, informative and complete** way, focusing on **reading and interpreting METAR, SPECI, TAF and SIGMET** from official DECEA sources (mainly **AISWEB** and **REDEMET**).

## Objective

This manual aims to standardise the interpretation of weather messages and aeronautical notices, reducing ambiguity, improving situational awareness and supporting decision-making (planning, alternates, minima, trends and convective risk).

## About this Manual

This manual was created according to the topics covered in the official DECEA and ICAO documents dealing with aeronautical meteorology, OPMET messages (METAR, SPECI, TAF) and NOTAM.

It is extremely important to read the official documents regularly and to consult the primary sources (AISWEB and REDEMET).

### Briefing routine (standard)

1. **NOTAM (AISWEB)**
   - Validity (B/C), scope (A/Q), impact on runway, navaids, procedures.
2. **METAR/SPECI (REDEMET)**
   - Wind (including gusts/variation), visibility/RVR, phenomena, ceiling, QNH.
3. **TAF (REDEMET)**
   - Operating window: worst case in **TEMPO**, **PROB**, **BECMG**, **FM** groups.
4. **Operational decision**
   - Minima, alternates, fuel, departure/arrival window, convective risk.

!!! note "Golden rule"
    **The METAR rules the "now".**  
    **The TAF rules the trend**, but always compare it with a recent update (SPECI, radar/satellite, local trend).

### Quick heuristics (no "guesswork")

- If **T/Td** are close (small spread), pay more attention to **BR/FG** and dropping visibility.
- If there is **TS** (thunderstorm), treat it as an operational risk (CB/gusts/wind shear).
- If there is **low BKN/OVC**, think **ceiling** (impacts IFR, approach, alternate).
- If there is **VRB**, **xxxVyyy**, **G** (gusts), think crosswind component, unstable approach and change of runway in use.

    !!! info "Wind notations"
        - **VRB**: Variable wind (no predominant direction, usually when speed < 3 kt).
        - **xxxVyyy**: Wind direction variation. Example: `180V240` means the wind varies between 180° and 240°. Indicates instability in wind direction.
        - **G**: Gusts. Example: `25015G25KT` means wind from 250° at 15 kt with gusts up to 25 kt. Indicates a sudden and significant variation in wind speed.

## How to use this manual

- Start with the **Overview** (above) to understand the operational flow.
- Go to **METAR and SPECI** for decoding and examples.
- Then **TAF** for reading terminal forecasts and change groups.
- Use **NOTAM** to understand structure, meanings and operational impact.
- Use the **Checklist** for the briefing routine.
- See the **Glossary** for abbreviations and technical terms.

## Scope

This manual covers the operational interpretation of weather messages and aeronautical notices for flights in normal conditions. For adverse situations, emergencies or specific cases, consult the specific regulations, AIP, charts and operator instructions.

!!! warning "Important"
    This manual does not replace regulations, AIP, charts, RBAC/ICA/ICAO, or operator instructions. It is an operational support guide.
