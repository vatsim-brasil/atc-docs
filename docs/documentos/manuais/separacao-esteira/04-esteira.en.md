# Wake Turbulence

## What wake turbulence is

Every wing that lifts an aircraft leaves two vortices behind it, one at each tip, which slowly descend and spread. The heavier the aircraft, the stronger they are. A smaller aircraft that enters these vortices suffers three effects[^1]:

- **violent rolling**, which may exceed the aircraft's control authority to resist it, and is the greatest danger;
- **loss of height** or of rate of climb;
- **structural stress**.

The risk is greatest close to the ground. On approach and take-off, the aircraft is slow, low and has little margin of speed, thrust and height to recover[^1].

Wake turbulence is invisible. No one can say precisely where it is[^2]. That is why the rules do not ask you to see it: they ask you to keep **enough distance or time** for it to dissipate or descend before the next aircraft passes.

### Helicopters

Helicopters also produce vortices, proportionally more intense than those of aeroplanes of the same weight. When hovering or air taxiing, the rotor blows high-speed air out to about **three times the rotor diameter**. Keep helicopters doing this clear of **LIGHT** aircraft[^3].

## Categories

Wake turbulence minima depend on each aircraft's category, defined by certificated maximum take-off mass[^4]:

| Category | Letter | Maximum take-off mass | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SUPER** | `J` | Types listed in Doc 8643 | `A388` |
| **HEAVY** | `H` | 136,000 kg or more | `B77W`, `B744`, `B789`, `A332`, `A359` |
| **MEDIUM** | `M` | More than 7,000 kg and less than 136,000 kg | `A320`, `B738`, `E195`, `AT76`, `B752` |
| **LIGHT** | `L` | 7,000 kg or less | `C172`, `PA34`, `C208`, `BE20` |

The category of each type is in ICAO Doc 8643[^4] and comes in item 9 of the flight plan, after the type (`A320/M`, `B77W/H`, `A388/J`).

SUPER and HEAVY aircraft must say **"super"** or **"heavy"** immediately after the callsign on **initial contact** with each unit[^5].

> **\*Brasília Center, TAM 8084 heavy.**

!!! note "Wake turbulence groups"
    Annex IV of ICA 100-37 has a more detailed classification into seven groups (A to G), which also considers wingspan. It may only be used with DECEA approval[^6] and is not applied on the network. Use the four categories.

## When to apply

Wake turbulence minima exist to reduce the hazard of wake turbulence and do not replace the normal minima. **The larger of the two always applies.** If the IFR minimum is larger than the wake minimum, apply the IFR minimum[^7].

Besides the cases defined on this page, you may apply a wake minimum in **any situation** where you see a possible wake hazard, even if no specific rule requires it[^8].

## With ATS surveillance

With ATS surveillance, wake minima are **distances**, measured on the screen like the radar minimum[^9]:

| Preceding | Following | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SUPER (`J`) | HEAVY (`H`) | **6 NM** |
| SUPER (`J`) | MEDIUM (`M`) | **7 NM** |
| SUPER (`J`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **8 NM** |
| HEAVY (`H`) | HEAVY (`H`) | **4 NM** |
| HEAVY (`H`) | MEDIUM (`M`) | **5 NM** |
| HEAVY (`H`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **6 NM** |
| MEDIUM (`M`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **5 NM** |

Pairs not in the table (MEDIUM behind MEDIUM, or any aircraft behind a LIGHT) have no wake minimum. The radar minimum applies to them.

These minima apply **on approach, on departure and en route below FL 240**, and only when[^10]:

- the following aircraft follows the preceding aircraft's track **at the same altitude or less than 1,000 ft below**;
- the following aircraft crosses the preceding aircraft's track **at the same altitude or less than 1,000 ft below**; or
- both use the **same runway**, or parallel runways **less than 760 m** apart.

Wake turbulence descends. That is why the rule only looks at aircraft at the same altitude or up to 1,000 ft **below** the other. An aircraft above the other's track, or more than 1,000 ft below it, does not need the wake minimum.

!!! tip "In practice"
    The only wake minima above the 5 NM radar minimum are those of a LIGHT behind a HEAVY (**6 NM**) and those of any aircraft behind an `A388` (**6, 7 and 8 NM**). On the Guarulhos final, with `B77W` and `A320` alternating, the wake minimum equals the radar minimum (5 NM), but the margin for compression on final disappears. Plan for 6 NM or more.

## On the runway and without surveillance

Without surveillance, and in the tower, wake minima are **times**. They apply between aircraft using the same runway or nearby runways, and do not depend on the tower having radar.

### Landings

An aircraft landing behind a heavier one needs[^11]:

| Preceding | Following | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SUPER (`J`) | HEAVY (`H`) | **2 min** |
| SUPER (`J`) | MEDIUM (`M`) | **3 min** |
| SUPER (`J`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **4 min** |
| HEAVY (`H`) | MEDIUM (`M`) | **2 min** |
| HEAVY (`H`) or MEDIUM (`M`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **3 min** |

### Departures

An aircraft departing behind a heavier one needs[^12]:

| Preceding | Following | Full length | From an intersection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SUPER (`J`) | HEAVY (`H`) | **2 min** | **3 min** |
| SUPER (`J`) | MEDIUM (`M`) or LIGHT (`L`) | **3 min** | **4 min** |
| HEAVY (`H`) | MEDIUM (`M`) or LIGHT (`L`) | **2 min** | **3 min** |
| MEDIUM (`M`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **2 min** | **3 min** |

The "full length" column applies when both aircraft are using[^13]:

- the same runway;
- parallel runways less than 760 m apart;
- crossing runways, if the projected flight path of the second aircraft crosses that of the first at the same altitude or less than 1,000 ft below; or
- parallel runways 760 m or more apart, with the same flight path crossing condition.

The "from an intersection" column applies when the following aircraft departs from an **intermediate** part of the same runway, or of a parallel runway less than 760 m away. It starts its take-off roll further along and gets airborne closer to the point where the preceding aircraft rotated. That is why the minimum goes up by 1 minute[^14].

!!! example "Close parallel runways"
    The two Guarulhos runways are about 375 m apart, less than 760 m. For wake turbulence purposes, they are **the same runway**. An `A320` departing from 09R just after a `B77W` departed from 09L needs the same 2 minutes it would need on the same runway.

### Displaced landing threshold

With a displaced landing threshold and crossing flight paths, an aircraft **departing** after a heavier one has **landed**, or **landing** after a heavier one has **departed**, needs the same times as a full-length departure: **2 min** (`H` behind `J`, `M` or `L` behind `H`, `L` behind `M`) or **3 min** (`M` or `L` behind `J`)[^15].

### Opposite direction

When a heavier aircraft makes a **low approach** or a **missed approach**, and a lighter one will **take off** on the same runway in the opposite direction, or **land** in the opposite direction on the same runway or on a parallel runway less than 760 m away, apply[^16]:

| Heavier | Lighter | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SUPER (`J`) | HEAVY (`H`) | **3 min** |
| SUPER (`J`) | MEDIUM (`M`) or LIGHT (`L`) | **4 min** |
| HEAVY (`H`) | MEDIUM (`M`) or LIGHT (`L`) | **3 min** |
| MEDIUM (`M`) | LIGHT (`L`) | **3 min** |

!!! note "Good practice"
    In the tower, note the time the heavier aircraft **gets airborne** (or crosses the threshold, when landing), and only clear the next one after the wake time. Tell the pilot why they are waiting. On the network, they do not always know they need to wait and may find the delay odd with the runway clear.

## When wake separation is not required

Control **does not need** to apply wake separation in two cases[^17]:

- **VFR landing** on the same runway behind a SUPER, HEAVY or MEDIUM aircraft;
- **IFR on a visual approach**, after reporting the preceding aircraft **in sight** and being instructed to **follow it and maintain own separation**.

In these cases, and whenever you consider it necessary, you **must** issue a **wake turbulence caution**. From then on, it is up to the pilot to decide whether the spacing from the heavier aircraft is acceptable and, if not, to request more separation[^18].

> **PUA 646, cleared visual approach runway 35, maintain own separation from preceding B747, caution wake turbulence.**

## Cautions and pilot requests

The wake turbulence caution gives the pilot what they need to protect themselves: the type of aircraft generating the wake and where it is[^19].

> **PT KTU, caution, wake turbulence B747, twelve o'clock, 7 miles, passing FL 120 climbing.**
>
> **PT ABC, caution, wake turbulence from Lear Jet departing to Campinas.**

SUPER aircraft call for extra attention[^20]:

- Warn the aircraft following a SUPER that is **climbing or descending within 15 NM** of it.
- If a pilot requests a **lateral offset**, more separation or another level to avoid the wake of a SUPER crossing their route, accommodate it whenever possible.
