# Introduction

## Overview

A helicopter does things no aeroplane does. It stops in the air, takes off from an apron, lands on a helipad in the middle of the aerodrome, flies slow and low and follows its own corridors over highways and rivers. For the controller, that is both a help and a trap. It helps because a helicopter fits into gaps no aeroplane would. It is a trap because the habit of treating everything as an aeroplane leads to clearances that make no sense, or to forgetting the rotor downwash next to a parked single-engine aircraft.

This manual is for anyone who will work helicopters on Vatsim Brasil, at each rating:

- on the **ground (GND, S1)**, which moves the helicopter around the manoeuvring area;
- at the **tower (TWR, S2)**, which clears landings and take-offs, on the runway and away from it;
- at **approach (APP, S3)**, which handles helicopters on the Helicopter Special Routes (REH), where they are published.

It covers routine control: the helicopter that arrives at, departs from or crosses a controlled aerodrome. At the end, it covers two specialities seen on the network, **offshore** operations and **public safety and air ambulance** operations, with the limits VATSIM sets on them.

## About this Manual

The regulatory basis is the **ICA 100-4**, Special Air Traffic Rules and Procedures for Helicopters[^1]. It has the definitions, the helicopter VFR rules, the aerodrome procedures, the REH, offshore operations and the helicopter phraseology. The **ICA 100-37**[^2] adds wake turbulence, rotor downwash and priorities. The **ICA 100-12**, Rules of the Air, gives the general VFR rules, the **MCA 100-27** glossary gives the definitions, and the **CIRCEA 100-111** and ANAC's **RBAC 90** feed the special operations page. The **MCA 100-16**[^3] gives the general phraseology; it has no helicopter section, so the specific transmissions come from chapter 7 of the ICA 100-4.

What is specific to the network follows VATSIM's **Code of Conduct**[^4], **Global Controller Administration Policy (GCAP)**[^5] and **Special Operations Policy**[^6].

Where real-world rules and network practice differ, the manual shows both and says which one applies on Vatsim Brasil. Technique that is not a rule is marked as good practice.

## Scope

This manual covers:

- what changes with helicopters for a controller: hovering, ground taxi, air-taxi, air-transit, helipads and occasional landing areas;
- the helicopter's own VFR rules, which are more flexible than the aeroplane's;
- GND and TWR work with helicopters at a controlled aerodrome;
- the REH and what APP does with helicopters on them, in general terms;
- offshore, public safety and air ambulance operations, and what VATSIM allows;
- phraseology, in Portuguese and English.

## Out of scope

This manual does not cover:

- each terminal area's REH, with its points, altitudes and frequencies: these are on the [terminal](../../../MOP/terminais/index.en.md) pages and in each region's AIC;
- flying the helicopter and helipad certification, which belong to ANAC;
- separation in general, covered in the [Separation and Wake Turbulence Manual](../separacao-esteira/index.md);
- helicopter IFR in detail: to the controller, it is almost always an ordinary category A IFR flight.

!!! warning "Important"
    Content intended for flight simulation only. For real-world operations, always use the current official publications, aeronautical charts and AIS and ATS channels.

## How to use this manual

1. Read **Fundamentals** for the vocabulary and what a helicopter can do. It applies to every rating.
2. If you are **S1**, read **Ground Control**.
3. If you are **S2**, also read **Tower**.
4. If you are **S3**, also read **Approach and REH**.
5. Everyone should read **Special Operations** before handling an offshore, police or air ambulance flight.
6. Look up the **Phraseology** and go over your rating's **Quick Checklist** before opening the position.

!!! tip "Further reading"
    Wake turbulence and weight categories are in the [Separation and Wake Turbulence Manual](../separacao-esteira/04-esteira.en.md). Each terminal area's REH are on the [terminal](../../../MOP/terminais/index.en.md) pages, in the REH map layer and the "Visual navigation documents" box.
